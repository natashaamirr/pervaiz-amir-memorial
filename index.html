In Memory

Dr. Pervaiz Amir

March 23, 1956  –  April 28, 2026
Economist. Water and climate expert. Horseman. Father.

Dr. Pervaiz Amir spent his life working on the issues he believed would shape Pakistan’s future: water, climate change and agriculture. He advised the World Bank, served on Pakistan’s Prime Minister’s Task Force on Climate Change, represented Pakistan at international climate conferences, and spent decades warning the country about the seriousness of its water crisis. He was never afraid to tell hard truths, especially when it came to protecting Pakistan’s future.

Outside his work, Pervaiz had a passion for flying and horses. He had immense patience, loved vintage things, and was an expert at bargaining. He was always in mentor mode: ready to share what he knew, offer advice, solve a problem, or help someone find their way. He was also the person everyone turned to when something needed fixing, a camera, laptop, car, or anything else. He would figure it out so nobody else had to worry.

Jhang was always where his heart was. A farm, horses and open land were the life he dreamed of.

He leaves behind a remarkable professional legacy, but those who knew him will remember just as much his generosity, patience, practicality and the quiet care he brought to everyday life.

He will be deeply missed.

The Little Things

One of his last voice notes to his daughter was about getting her camera fixed. He has left a very big hole in our hearts.

Horses

He loved horses. He said it came from the Prophet Muhammad's love for horses. That was where it started for him.

In 1997, he wrote and published Oriental Horses of Pakistan, a study of breeds, breeding, and the centuries-old tradition of tent pegging, carried with a foreword by a President of Pakistan.

“Dr. Pervaiz Amir, a true Renaissance man: an ace agriculture expert, insightful economist, passionate farmer, sharp analyst... with a heart that beats for horses.” Saleem Akhtar Rao, on receiving a copy of the book

From His Notebook

A Private List of Lessons

Found among his papers, in his own hand.

  1. Only concentrate on what you can control.
  2. If it is not your business, it is not your business.
  3. Live a virtuous life.
  4. Silence is a great asset.
  5. Humility is the greatest virtue.
  6. Learn each and every moment.
  7. Live in today: yesterday was a memory or a lesson, tomorrow may never come.
